# Status Compass — U.S. F-1 International Student Rules Research Assignment

You are conducting high-accuracy policy research for **Status Compass**, an educational planning application for F-1 international students in the United States.

Your job is to build a carefully verified, citation-backed knowledge base that helps students understand:

- what immigration-policy changes may affect them;
- why those changes were made;
- which dates deserve attention;
- how relevant dates are calculated;
- what facts affect the calculation;
- what actions may require preparation;
- which possible paths should be discussed with a Designated School Official (DSO);
- what remains uncertain or dependent on individual circumstances; and
- where every statement came from.

This product is not a legal-advice chatbot. It must not determine whether a student is maintaining lawful status or guarantee eligibility for an immigration benefit.

## 1. Research date and legal-status verification

Treat the research date and time as:

- Date: August 19, 2026
- Time zone: America/New_York

Every source and rule must be evaluated as of this date.

The main focus is the Department of Homeland Security final rule:

- Title: **Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors, and Representatives of Foreign Information Media**
- Federal Register citation: **91 FR 44976**
- Document number: **2026-14439**
- Publication date: **July 17, 2026**
- Scheduled effective date: **September 15, 2026**
- DHS docket: **ICEB-2025-0001**
- RIN: **1653-AA95**

Do not assume that the rule will become effective merely because September 15, 2026 is printed as the effective date.

Before describing it as active or certain, search for:

1. A Federal Register document changing its effective date.
2. A Federal Register document terminating or withdrawing the rule.
3. Congressional Review Act action.
4. Newly enacted legislation affecting the rule.
5. DHS, ICE, SEVP, USCIS, CBP, or Department of State implementation guidance.
6. A federal-court complaint, temporary restraining order, preliminary injunction, stay, final order, or appellate decision.
7. Any correction or amendment to document 2026-14439.
8. Updated eCFR text.
9. Any conflict between the final rule, agency guidance, court orders, and secondary reporting.

Assign one of these statuses:

- `proposed`
- `published_pending_effective`
- `effective`
- `effective_partially`
- `delayed`
- `stayed_by_court`
- `enjoined`
- `terminated`
- `withdrawn`
- `superseded`
- `status_uncertain`

If the status cannot be established conclusively, use `status_uncertain` and explain exactly what remains unknown.

## 3. Required main-rule research

Read the complete final rule, including its preamble, responses to comments, regulatory text, transition provisions, definitions, dates, examples, and stated rationale.

Investigate and explain, without oversimplification:

5. The objections or concerns raised by commenters.
6. DHS’s responses to those objections.
7. The scheduled effective date and every qualification attached to it.
8. The difference between publication, scheduled effectiveness, actual effectiveness, and implementation.
9. Who is covered: F-1, F-2, J-1, J-2, I, or other classifications.
10. Which parts are relevant specifically to F-1 students.
11. The treatment of students admitted before the effective date with `D/S`.
12. The treatment of students admitted or readmitted after the effective date.
13. How a fixed admission period is determined.
14. The maximum admission period and situations that may produce a shorter period.
15. The relationship among the I-94, I-20, program dates, visa expiration, EAD, SEVIS record, and admission period.
16. Transition periods and their starting and ending conditions.
17. Program completion and departure/grace periods.
18. Extension-of-stay procedures.
19. Form I-539 requirements, if applicable.
20. Recommended or legally required filing windows.
21. Timely filed extension applications and authorized-stay implications.
22. What happens while an extension request is pending.
23. Biometrics or other application requirements, if relevant.
24. OPT and STEM OPT treatment.
25. Post-completion OPT and STEM OPT EAD dates.
26. Cap-gap treatment, if affected.
27. CPT, if affected.
28. Change of educational level.
29. Transfer to another school.
30. Changing programs or majors.

33. International travel and readmission after the effective date.
34. Reentry during OPT or STEM OPT.
35. Reinstatement and change-of-status scenarios.

38. Any special country, program, school, accreditation, national-interest, or reciprocity limits.
39. Any exceptions, exemptions, discretionary provisions, or unresolved agency questions.
40. Any implementation guidance schools and DSOs are still waiting to receive.

Clearly distinguish:

- a legal deadline;
- an admission expiration date;
- an I-20 program date;
- an EAD expiration date;
- a filing window;
- a transition-period endpoint;
- a grace/departure period;
- a school-recommended preparation date;
- and a Status Compass reminder date.

Never present a suggested preparation date as a government deadline.

## 5. Deadline and timeline calculation research

Status Compass will ask a student for a limited set of non-identifying facts:

- whether the I-94 shows `D/S` or a fixed date;
- I-94 admit-until date, if present;
- I-20 program start date;
- I-20 program end date;
- current academic stage;
- whether the student is preparing for, applying for, or participating in OPT or STEM OPT;
- EAD start and expiration dates, if applicable;
- planned international travel and expected reentry date;
- whether an application is pending, without asking for a receipt number;
- desired next step: continue study, complete program, practical training, transfer, travel, change level, or prepare for a DSO meeting.

For every deadline or timeline calculation, provide:

- the student inputs required;
- the controlling official date;
- the legal or policy basis;
- an exact deterministic formula where one exists;
- inclusive/exclusive day-counting rules;
- weekend or federal-holiday treatment, if stated;
- before/on/after boundary behavior;
- missing-input behavior;
- whether the result is a legal deadline or an informational reminder;
- what must be confirmed by a DSO;
- official citation;
- minimal supporting quotation;
- and at least three test cases.

Do not invent a numerical deadline when the authority uses language such as “recommended,” “reasonable,” “promptly,” or “up to.”

For each formula, distinguish:

```text
official_deadline = derived strictly from authority
preparation_reminder = optional product reminder
dso_review_date = suggested conversation date, not a legal deadline
```

If Status Compass uses a preparation buffer—for example, reminding a student 30 days before something—mark it as:

- configurable;
- not legally required;
- not part of the federal rule; and
- designed only to encourage earlier DSO preparation.

## 6. “Why this is happening” requirement

The knowledge base must explain both the government rationale and the student impact.

For every major change include:

- `official_rationale`: DHS’s stated reason;
- `commenter_concerns`: important objections raised during rulemaking;
- `agency_response`: DHS’s response;
- `practical_student_impact`: what may change for a student;
- `why_this_result_appears`: why Status Compass would display the finding;
- `uncertainty`: what the application cannot conclude;
- `questions_for_dso`: questions the student should take to a DSO.

Do not convert the government’s policy rationale into an independent factual conclusion. Use wording such as:

> DHS states that the change is intended to…

rather than:

> This change prevents fraud.

## 7. Product safety requirements

All output must follow these boundaries:

- Educational information only.
- No determination of lawful status.
- No eligibility guarantee.
- No claim that a student is “safe,” “unsafe,” “legal,” or “illegal.”
- No percentage risk scores.

- No assumptions from missing data.
- No collection of names, SEVIS IDs, A-numbers, passport numbers, visa numbers, card numbers, or USCIS receipt numbers.
- Important decisions must be directed to a DSO or qualified immigration attorney.
- Each active rule must trace to at least one Tier 1 source.
- Secondary reports must never directly activate application logic.

Use attention labels only:

- `confirm_now`
